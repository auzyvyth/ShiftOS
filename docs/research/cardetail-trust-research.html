Method

How this was put together

Desk research across published product documentation, press material and industry UX studies for eight marketplaces, plus a full read of the current src/pages/CarDetailPage.jsx (3,948 lines) and DESIGN.md.

One honest limitation. This session's network policy blocked direct page fetches of carsome.my, cinch.co.uk and the other marketplaces, so the visual specifics below come from their own published documentation, press releases and my prior knowledge of these products — not from a live screenshot pass done today. The mechanics are well documented and stable; exact pixel treatments should be confirmed against your own inspo when you send it.

The finding

Trust is not a badge

Every marketplace that converts well does the same one thing: it answers the buyer's fear with something the buyer can check without having to believe the seller. Sites that feel untrustworthy are not short of trust signals — they are full of claims and short of evidence.

That distinction is the whole report. A green shield saying "Verified Dealer" is a claim. A scan of the Puspakom certificate the buyer can open is evidence. They cost about the same to build and they are not remotely the same thing.

The second finding matters for your specific worry about looking "vibe coded". Stanford's web credibility work — three years, 4,500 people — found 46.1% of people judged a site's credibility partly on visual design: layout, typography, spacing, consistency. On e-commerce sites specifically, design-based credibility comments came up 46.2% of the time. So the feeling you have is real and it is measurable: inconsistency reads as "assembled quickly", and "assembled quickly" reads as "might not be a real business".

The buyer

What a Malaysian used-car buyer is actually afraid of

Trust design starts from fear, not from features. These seven are the ones that show up over and over in Malaysian buying guides and consumer-protection material. Each one has a specific piece of evidence that kills it — and only that piece of evidence kills it.

"It's been in a big accident and they patched it."
Kill it with: photographed condition disclosure — the actual dent, on the actual car, marked on a body diagram. Not the word "excellent".
"It's a flood car, or the mileage was rolled back."
Kill it with: a third-party record — service invoices with dates and readings, a named inspection, an insurance claim check.
"It's still under HP and they can't actually transfer it."
Kill it with: a loan settlement / clearance letter shown as a document, plus an explicit encumbrance state. You already store encumbrance_status on stock units.
"Is this a sambung bayar trap?"
Kill it with: saying it out loud. Sambung bayar is illegal under the Hire Purchase Act 1967; a page that names the risk instead of hiding it gains more trust than one that says nothing.
"I'll pay the deposit and never see it again."
Kill it with: written refund terms, at the exact moment you ask for the deposit — not in a footer, not in a T&C modal.
"The price is inflated and I'm being played."
Kill it with: a market comparison that shows its sample size and its method. A confident verdict from a hidden method is worse than no verdict.
"Who even is this seller? Are they real?"
Kill it with: real-world identity — trading address, landline, company registration, years trading, review count. Stanford's #1 credibility guideline, and the biggest hole in your page today.
The model

The trust ladder

This is the most useful thing to come out of the research. Everything a car page can say sits on one of five rungs, and the rungs are not close to equal. One item from rung 4 is worth more than ten from rung 1. Judge every element on your page by which rung it sits on.

1

Claim near worthless

"Well maintained." "Peace of mind." "Excellent condition." "Verified." Anyone can type it, so it proves nothing — and in volume it actively signals a template. This is the rung generic dealer sites live on, and it is the rung a buyer subconsciously recognises as filler.

2

Structured self-report weak

Mileage, previous owners, auction grade, warranty months — filled into real fields. Still the seller's word, but the structure itself signals a system behind the listing rather than a free-text ad. Cheap, necessary, not sufficient.

3

A document the buyer can open real

The Puspakom B5 certificate. The service invoice. The loan clearance letter. The company registration. The buyer verifies it themselves, so belief in you is no longer required. This is the first rung that actually moves someone.

4

A disclosed flaw strongest cheap signal

A photographed scratch. "AC compressor is noisy." A body diagram with real marks on it. Counter-intuitive and enormously powerful: a seller who volunteers the bad news is understood to be hiding nothing else. Carvana built a company on this — 64 camera angles, 12x zoom, and a human annotation team whose entire job is labelling imperfections as clickable hotspots on the 360 tour.

5

Money on the line survives a sceptic

A return window. A warranty with stated terms. A refundable deposit. Escrow. cinch: 14-day money back, free 90-day warranty, free collection on return. CarMax: 10-day return. Carvana: 7 days. Carsome: 5-day money-back plus a 1-year warranty. The only rung that a cynical buyer cannot argue with.

Read your own page against this and the picture is clear: you are strong on rung 2, decent on rung 3, almost absent on rung 4 for non-recon cars, and completely absent on rung 5.

Patterns

The design language, cherry-picked

Twelve patterns that recur across the good ones. These are the transferable part — the grammar, not the features.

Evidence sits next to the number it defends

None of the strong pages have a "trust section". The market verdict sits under the price. The warranty sits next to the CTA. The inspection date sits next to the mileage. A dedicated trust block is where trust goes to be ignored.

Every computed claim carries its sample size and date

"Based on 12 similar listings, updated 3 Aug." The moment you show the working, the verdict becomes checkable instead of asserted.

Named, numbered, countable

"175-point inspection" works because a buyer can imagine counting it. "Thoroughly inspected" is an adjective and adjectives are free. Wherever you can attach a number to a process, do.

Show the flaw with the same production value as the feature

Same photo quality, same card treatment, no apologetic tone, no small grey text. Disclosure hidden in a footnote reads as a legal cover; disclosure presented confidently reads as honesty.

Documents are objects to open, not badges to admire

A thumbnail of the actual certificate with a real filename beats a green tick every time. The tick is rung 1; the file is rung 3.

Prove there is a real organisation

Stanford's top guideline, unchanged in twenty years: trading address, landline, a photo of the premises, a registration number. This is the cheapest large credibility win available to you.

State the conditions, don't bury them

cinch publishes the mileage cap on its money-back guarantee. Limits make a promise look like it came from a legal department, which is to say from a real company.

One primary action

A stack of four differently-coloured buttons is the single most reliable visual tell of a template. Your own CLAUDE.md already bans it; the page hasn't caught up.

Neutral voice inside evidence zones

Sales language contaminates a trust block. "Drive with peace of mind" inside a warranty card converts the card from rung 3 to rung 1 with one sentence.

Consistency is read as competence

One spacing scale, one radius set, one accent, one type ramp. Visitors cannot articulate why a page feels amateur, but mismatched greys and three button styles is why.

Price completeness

What is in the number and what is not — road tax, insurance, JPJ transfer, processing. Pricing transparency is the most consistently cited consumer demand in every automotive study in the set.

Recency

"Listed 3 days ago", "price updated", "last inspected". Inventory with no timestamps reads as scraped or fake. You already have listedDays — it deserves more than 10px grey.

Diagnosis

Why your page can read as vibe coded

Nine specific things, all in src/pages/CarDetailPage.jsx. None of these are the whole feeling on their own; together they are exactly it.

Self-attested verification

rung 1 in a rung 3 costume

The Puspakom row prints "Inspection verified by the dealer." The dealer verifying their own car is circular — it reads as a template that wanted the word "verified" in it. Either name the actual verifier (Puspakom branch, report number) or drop the line; the B5/B7 dates above it are already doing honest work.

src/pages/CarDetailPage.jsx:288

Filler benefit copy inside a trust card

rung 1

The warranty banner's sub-line is "Covered by the dealer · drive with peace of mind". "Peace of mind" is the most common stock phrase in generic dealer templates — it is the phrase a sceptical buyer's eye slides straight past. Replace it with terms: what is covered, mileage cap, how to claim.

src/pages/CarDetailPage.jsx:196

Condition disclosure only exists for recon cars

biggest single gap

The DamageMap only renders when isRecon is true, on both the mobile and desktop paths. Every local used car — the bulk of the marketplace — currently ships with zero condition disclosure. That is the highest-value rung on the ladder, the component already exists, and it is switched off for most of your inventory.

src/pages/CarDetailPage.jsx:2250 (mobile) · src/pages/CarDetailPage.jsx:3042 (desktop) · src/components/DamageMap.jsx

No real-world dealer identity

Stanford #1

The sidebar shows a name, an avatar and a shield reading "Verified Dealer" — and nothing behind it. No trading address, no company or SSM number, no landline, no map, no "trading since", no total sales. And "Verified Dealer" with no definition of what was verified is itself a rung 1 claim wearing rung 3 clothing.

src/pages/CarDetailPage.jsx:3453 (dealer topbar) · :3475 (verified shield)

Social proof is below the fold, twice

wrong place

ReviewsSection sits after Performance, Running Costs and Location on both breakpoints. The decision is made at the price block; reviews sitting five sections below it are decorative. A rating chip belongs beside the dealer name.

src/pages/CarDetailPage.jsx:3414 (desktop) · src/pages/CarDetailPage.jsx:2544 (mobile)

The deposit ask has no protection language

highest-anxiety moment

"RM X deposit to reserve" renders as an 11px muted centered caption. This is the single most frightening sentence on the page for a Malaysian buyer, and it is styled as the least important text in the sidebar. What happens if the loan is rejected? Is it refundable? Who holds it?

src/pages/CarDetailPage.jsx:3527

Eight competing actions in one sidebar

template tell

Book a Viewing (red), WhatsApp (green), Call (grey), Financing calculator, seller storefront link, Chat with [first name] (green again), View all listings, Go to dealer's page. Two green buttons do near-identical jobs. This breaks your own anti-slop rule in CLAUDE.md — one primary action visible, the long tail behind an overflow — and a wall of buttons is precisely the thing that reads as generated.

src/pages/CarDetailPage.jsx:3530–3600

Theme leaks: dark-theme colours on the light surface

credibility hygiene

DESIGN.md warns about this and says it has bitten twice. Three live cases: the SAVE badge text is #f87171 on a white card; the salesman line "Independent Agent · XDrive" is hardcoded #1e293b, near-invisible on the dark subdomain theme; the Call button border is rgba(255,255,255,0.1), which vanishes entirely on the light theme so the button loses its edge. Washed-out and edgeless elements are the most reliable amateur tell a visitor can spot without knowing why.

src/pages/CarDetailPage.jsx:3513 · :3593 · :3542

The market verdict has no method and no sample floor

rung 2 pretending to be rung 3

MarketPriceTag will happily print "Below market · based on 3 similar listings" in confident green. A strong verdict off a three-car sample is worse than no verdict — if a buyer checks and disagrees, every other number on the page loses credibility with it. Cars.com's badges work because the method is published; suppress the band below a sample threshold and link a one-paragraph explanation.
